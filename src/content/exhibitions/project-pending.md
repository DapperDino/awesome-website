**Context**

**COLAB** was a six-week experimental and planning period ending in a residency at **Surface Gallery** (25-30 April 2023).

The fine art course’s year group was split up into groups of roughly 5, who then created their own projects as mini collectives. My group included Amy Pallent, Chloe Fox, Abigail Gordon, Charlotte Simpson, Max Geisinger, and of course myself.

My group created a fictional newspaper called ***From Nottingham with Love***, which we created articles for and printed out. In the exhibition we installed a newsroom where, at intervals at the opening, our collective’s members would be news presenters. During the rest of the exhibition opening, we were reporters: gathering information from visitors and recording the event with photography.

**Influences and Inspirations** – (text taken from university notes)

**Fax-Bak** (Artist collective): a group who marked gallery press releases like teachers at primary schools to fax back to the gallery. This editorial **“fact to fiction”** style of writing was an interesting expansion from the evolving narrative we were exploring. From developing **Brother’s Grimm Tales** through memory, like the children’s game **Chinese whispers**, the editing style allowed us to choose how we wanted the text to sound. The experiment turned out to be a turning point in our process because from this we used newspapers with true stories and turned them into fictional ones.

The newspaper idea is timely because the media nowadays bombards us with information, and we don’t know what sources to trust. Fake news, tabloids with eye-catching titles, and the current upsetting news on the ongoing war (at the time in Ukraine) made us want to respond by satirizing it. The process of making the newspaper turned into a type of **humorous coping mechanism**.

Our “backstory” as news reporters and readers became our concept and took inspiration from the news. **Barbara Kruger** was a big inspiration throughout COLAB, especially her work *All Violence is an Illustration of a Pathetic Stereotype* (1991). By using the entire height of the wall in Surface Gallery like Kruger, with the advantage of the risograph printer’s speed in mass production, we recreated the background that is often used in the news.

We dressed up in suits like **news presenters and reporters** for the exhibition opening. Through discussion with the group this evolved into doing a performance of news media alongside the newspaper, which included the typical end of a news programme, where they say goodnight and start talking amongst themselves while shuffling papers. We evolved this idea to highlight this aimless task by presenting the news through mouthing “Rhubarb” and nodding at each other while reading from blank pieces of paper. This would highlight the **pointlessness of the newspaper itself**, showing that by **reading our newspaper as truth you would be fooling yourself**. We added to this by asking the visitors to the exhibition to **vandalise our newspaper wall**. The behaviour of visitors suggested that the news is taken with a sense of indifference (we had uncollected newspapers leftover), whereas the act of vandalism attracts the interest of visitors (the wall was covered in doodles and graffiti). This was the reaction we were hoping for because it evidences what we have been investigating through the “newspaper”.


**Artist’s Reflections**

The interviewing of people with a paint brush (in place of a microphone) was challenging because my previous performance-based work had been non-interactive. Chloe Fox, Abigail Gordon, and I went around the entire exhibition commentating on the artworks, claiming to “report back to the studio” with any “scoops” gained from speaking to the public. It was a surprisingly enjoyable process because the group dressed in suits to emphasise our characters and performance.
